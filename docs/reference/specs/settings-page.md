# Settings page

`GET /settings` is the dashboard section where a customer configures what Switchboard already lets them configure, and sees what only `config.yaml` can change. It is an adapter over the command registry and nothing more ([record 0041](../../decisions/0041-the-settings-page-is-a-surface-over-the-registry-and-configures-the-shared-tiers.md)): every tab's seed is the answer of registry commands invoked as the viewer, every write the page makes is one `POST /api/<group>.<verb>` ([command-registry.md](command-registry.md), [dashboard-routes.md](../dashboard-routes.md)), and the page carries no rule of its own about who may do what. Three tabs: **MCPs** (`mcp list` as a table, an add form, a connect link, a remove button; [mcp-tools.md](mcp-tools.md) items 13 to 19), **Channels** (the index of configured channels, then one channel's scope as a form over `config show|set|clear|instructions channel`; [routing-and-config.md](routing-and-config.md) items 5, 12, 24) and **Installation** (a read-only projection of the running `config.yaml` and the capabilities that are on; [capabilities.md](capabilities.md)).

The dashboard configures the **shared tiers**, org and channel, and the viewer's **own**: a session **linked** to its Slack person writes the person's scope ([record 0042](../../decisions/0042-a-dashboard-session-is-the-person-its-email-names-identity-not-authority.md): the Access email names one active Slack user, resolved once in the gate); an unlinked one writes its own `access:<sub>` scope, the identity the dashboard's chat requests its runs as ([record 0043](../../decisions/0043-the-home-page-is-a-chat-the-browser-is-a-channel-and-a-turn-is-a-run.md)), so what a session sets is what its runs read. The one caller with no `me` is a service token (`access:svc:…`): no run is ever requested as one, and the config and MCP handlers refuse its `me` write by the data (routing-and-config item 5, mcp-tools item 14).

## Behavior

1. **Routes.** `/settings` opens on MCPs where the `mcp` capability is on, else on Channels; `/settings/mcps` (with `?channel=<id>` to list one channel's tier beside org and own), `/settings/channels`, `/settings/channels/<channel id>` (percent-encoded or not; a platform-namespaced id like `slack:C…`) and `/settings/installation` are the tabs. Anything else under `/settings` is not a route. The page sits behind the dashboard's one identity gate like `/runs*`; a method other than GET is a 405; a registry that throws is a 502 with a capped reason, never a 500.
2. **The seed is the registry's answer, as the viewer** — the viewer being the caller `resolveAccessActor` resolves, linked to its Slack person when the session's email names one ([record 0042](../../decisions/0042-a-dashboard-session-is-the-person-its-email-names-identity-not-authority.md)); the seed carries `asUser` then, and the MCPs tab's `me` tier (defaulted when no channel is open) is the person's, their rows marked as the viewer's own and writable; unlinked, `me` is the session's own tier and its own rows are the writable ones. The MCPs tab's rows are `mcp list` (with `--channel` when one is named), the Channels tab's index is `config overrides` and its open channel is `config show --channel <id>`, each invoked through the bound registry with the `/api` caller for the gate's identity (`callerFor`), so the page can see exactly what the same identity could read on the CLI. A refused or unavailable command puts its sentence on the seed in place of the rows (`unavailable`, `refused`) and the page shows it as is. The open channel's scope is `channelScopeView`: `config show` without the viewer's own user scope and without every `mcpServers` map (the MCPs tab shows servers through `McpServerView`, which carries no credential name).
3. **Installation is a projection by allow-list.** `installationSettings(config, capabilities)` writes every row out by name (`row(key, configured, default, how, note)`), nothing iterated off the config object except `defaults.models` and `defaults.efforts`, whose keys are preset names; each row carries the configured value or the default marked `isDefault`, and `how` says whether a channel overrides it at run time (`runtime`, the `defaults.*` rows) or `config.yaml` with `deploy config` and a restart changes it (`config`). The capability list names each capability's block in words, never an env var. The rendered view holds no `*Env` name, no URL, no `SHOUTING_CASE` identifier and no name from the deploy secrets manifest, whatever the config holds. The tab is gated on `config:read`.
4. **`canWrite` is the handler's question, asked early.** The seed carries `canWrite.org = authorize(actor, "mcp:write", config-scope org)`, `canWrite.channel = authorize(actor, "mcp:write", config-scope channel)` (false without a channel) on the MCPs tab and `authorize(actor, "config:write", config-scope channel)` on an open channel: the same calls the `mcp.*` and `config.*` handlers make. The page uses them to disable controls and say why; it decides nothing, and a write forced through anyway meets the handler's refusal.
5. **Every write is one command.** Add posts `mcp.add` with `name`, `url`, `scope` (`me` — the viewer's own tier, defaulted when no channel is open — or `org`, or `channel` with `channel`), `agents` joined by commas and `auth` when chosen; Connect posts `mcp.connect` and Remove (after a confirm) posts `mcp.remove`, each naming the row's tier; Probe reads `mcp.show`. Save posts `config.set` with `scope: channel`, `channel` and only the filled fields (a `config set` patches; an empty field leaves the stored value alone); the instructions box posts `config.instructions` with its text, an empty text clearing them; Clear (after a confirm) posts `config.clear`. A connect link comes back from `add` and `connect` and is shown as a link to the Access-gated connect page; a credential never passes through this page.
6. **The tabs follow the capabilities; the way in is the header's cog.** Settings is chrome, not a section of the dashboard: every page's header carries the settings cog (`web/src/components/SettingsLink.vue`, a same-tab link to `/settings`, `aria-current="page"` only on the settings page) beside the docs link, and the phone menu lists it in the docs' group; the site nav's sections are unchanged. The MCPs tab exists only where `mcp` is on or the viewer is on it; Channels and Installation always. The page without a seed renders its empty state, never a crash.
7. **Channels tab.** *Never "no data"* (record 0041, accepted): the tab opens on the viewer's own settings — what a run they ask for outside any channel gets (`config show` without a channel: the installation defaults under their own scope, `ViewerSettingsView`), the defaults, and their scope or the sentence that nothing is theirs yet; a refusal of that read is shown as is beside the index. The index lists each channel that carries a scope with the names of its settings and where they come from (`config.yaml`, `runtime`, both), as links to the channel's page; an empty index says every channel runs on the defaults, and a **picker** opens any channel the viewer may read, by name (`config channels` → the seed's `pickable`: `#name`, a private one marked, the id in the option's tooltip; the bot's own channels plus the scoped ones), with an id field folded away under it for a channel the list cannot carry (a machine channel, an unlisted one) and the id field alone when nothing could be listed; a note says when the bot could not list its channels and only the scoped ones are offered. *Names, not ids* (record 0042, the dashboard reads names): the seed carries each channel's name without its hash beside its id (`channelName`, from the core's `NameDirectory` — `src/core/names.ts`, the Slack directory `src/channels/slackNames.ts` over the adapter's cached `conversations.info` and `users.info` lookups; the static one knows nothing), asked once per channel, concurrently, a failure or an unknown id leaving the id alone; the index rows, the open channel's heading and a refusal line read `#name` with the id in the tooltip (and beside the heading), and the id alone when there is no name. A page served without its seed renders an application error with a reload, never an empty state. The open channel shows the effective agent, model and effort for a run there, the form over its scope (agent, model, per-agent models, effort, per-agent efforts, the boundary's three axes, instructions), the restricted agents marked, and the admins hint when the viewer may not write.
8. **MCPs tab.** Rows show name, tier (a user row names its owner), URL (origin and path only, as `McpServerView` gives it), agents, auth, state (`connected`, `awaiting credential`, `pinned in config.yaml`), an **Added by** column that reads `you` for the viewer's own — the person's display name when the service resolved one, the id otherwise ([record 0042](../../decisions/0042-a-dashboard-session-is-the-person-its-email-names-identity-not-authority.md)); Connect appears on a runtime row whose auth is not `none`, Remove on any runtime row, both disabled without the tier's write right; a static row has neither. **Which rows** (record 0042): a viewer with the org write right sees every tier there is (the seed is `mcp list --all`, `allTiers: true`) and a **Promote** button on every person's runtime row that is not already shadowed, which asks first and posts `mcp.promote` with the row's name and owner, then shows the org's connect link (or reloads when `auth: none` connected at once). *Reading the table:* names, not ids — a user row's tier names its owner and `promoted from` names the person, through the view's `ownerName`/`promotedFromName`; the agents are the run page's colored agent chips (`AGENT_HUE`); a row a higher tier shadows carries a `shadowed by <tier>` mark and is dimmed; the action column has four fixed slots so buttons line up down the page. *The connect link opens in a new tab*, and while a link is out the page watches its row (one `mcp list` every few seconds, bounded by the link's ten-minute life) and reloads when the row's state changes — so closing the connect tab lands back on a current list; anyone else sees the org's, the open channel's and their own, plus the channel tiers of every channel whose config they may read — `config overrides` names them, one `mcp list --channel` each; a public channel, a granted one, or a private channel the viewer's linked person is in ([authorization.md](authorization.md) item 7) — and no Promote. The add form offers the org tier and the channel tier — the open channel's when one is open, else a picker of the channels the viewer may read appears in the form itself (`config channels`, the same `pickable`) and the add posts the picked channel; the channel field above the table is the same picker — an open channel the list does not carry stays an option, an id field is folded under it, and the id field alone when nothing could be listed; Add stays disabled for the channel tier until a channel is open or picked, the picked channel's write right being the handler's to decide. A channel tier's agents are limited to the self-serve presets. The open channel (`?channel=`) and every channel-tier row read `#name` when the directory knows the channel (`channelName` on the seed and on the row, the service's `resolveChannelName` being the same directory), the id otherwise and always in the tooltip.
